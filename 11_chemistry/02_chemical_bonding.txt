VALENCE ELECTRONS: electrons in outer shells in an atom. The ones involved in forming chemical bonds
BOND DISTANCE: distance between the centers of two atoms involved in a chemical bond
    Applicable to covalent and ionic bonds
No need to remember eq for lattice energy
LATTICE ENERGY: energy required to break an ionic compound in solid state into its individual gaseous ions
    Depends on the Coulomb potential and the crystal's geometry
Energy of formation defined in terms of the most stable states for each of the elements (e.g. Na solid, Cl gaseous)
COORDINATION NUMBER: number of cations surrounding an anion (or vice versa)
Each line in a "bond diagram" represents 2 shared electrons
There won't be 4 and 5 electron bonds in the 2nd period because there are no free orbitals (this is more than 8 electrons)
Polar = it has a dipole moment > 0
    Same atom = non-polar
Each bond has a dipole moment, total dipole moment is vector sum of individual moments for each bond in the molecule
    Individual dipole moments may be != 0, and total be == 0 (e.g. symmetry)
Lone pair = pair of valence electrons that are not shared (not part of any bond in the molecule)
Dotted line = para adentro, bold line = afuera
Only polar solvents can solve ionic compounds
Distance between atoms in a covalent bond is smaller for double/triple/... bonds
Resonant structures: structures that are also possible for a molecule. In reality, all present to some extent
Electron pairs tend to repulse each other, whether they are part of a bond or not => angular structures
    Electron pair geometry: the geometry of the pairs, e.g. tetrahedral
        When separating 4 regions of space: tetrahedral
        When separating 3 regions of space: trigonal planar
    Molecular geometry: the one for the molecule, e.g. angular for water, trigonal pyramidal for NH3
    The lone pair causes more repulsion than the bond-forming pairs
        E.g. NH3 is not an exact tetrahedral
In a benzene ring, we would expect distances between double bonds to be smaller, but they are all the same => resonant structures
Valence bond theory: a bond is interpreted as the overlap of several orbitals
Sigma bond: the overlapping occurs along the line joining atomic nuclei
Pi bond: the overlapping occurs outside the line joining atomic nuclei
Double bonds may contain only one sigma bond, the other has to be pi
To form a bond, you need an unpaired electron. E.g. even if C is 1s² 2s² 2p², a reconfiguration happens before bonding: becomes [He] 2s¹ 2p³
Hybridization theory: for CH4, we know that geometry is tetrahedral
    but if we try to apply valence bond theory, we wouldn't get tetrahedral: we have s¹ and p³ electrons
    Proposal: the resulting orbitals are neither s nor p, but a hybrid, and all equal (sp³)
    Also sp², if there are only 3 electrons hybridizing, and sp if there are only 2
    sp³ yields tetrahedral, sp² trigonal planar, sp linear. Look for electron pair geometry, not for molecular geometry!
    C2H2 (triple bond): C with H is sp, then 2 pi bonds and 1 sigma bond to form the triple bond
    sp³d: one electron from s to d => trigonal bipyramidal
    sp³d² => octahedral
Miscibility given by intermolecular forces: e.g. if you have dipole-dipole interactions they mix, but if you mix polar and non-polar they don't mix
H bond: H with N, O, F with a lone pair
H bonds increase melting/boiling points: stronger attractive forces

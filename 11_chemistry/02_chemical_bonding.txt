VALENCE ELECTRONS: electrons in outer shells in an atom. The ones involved in forming chemical bonds
INTRAMOLECULAR FORCES: electrostatic forces between atoms in the same molecule
INTERMOLECULAR FORCES: electrostatic forces between molecules
BOND DISTANCE: distance between the centers of two atoms involved in a chemical bond
    Applicable to both covalent and ionic bonds
BOND ANGLE: angle between the lines joining three atoms bond together
OCTET RULE: main-group elements tend to bond in a way that achieves 8 electrons in its outer shell
    Achieves an electronic configuration akin to a noble gas
IONIC BOND: bond in which an atom species donates electrons to another, forming anions and cations
    Usually between atoms with high electron affinity and atoms with low ionization energy (metal and non-metal)
    LATTICE ENERGY: energy required to break an ionic compound in solid state into its individual gaseous ions
        Depends on the Coulomb potential and the crystal's geometry
    BORN-HABER CYCLE: thermodynamic cycle to measure lattice energy indirectly
        ENTHALPY OF ATOMIZATION: energy required to break one mole of substance into its gaseous atoms
        ENTHALPY OF DISSOCIATION: energy required to break a covalent bond, with each resulting ion getting the same number of electrons
        Enthalpy of formation is < 0 (energetically favorable) because of lattice energy
    COORDINATION NUMBER: in an ionic crystal, number of cations surrounding an anion (or vice versa)
        E.g. tetrahedral crystals have coordination number = 4
    Properties of ionic compounds
        Solids that form crystals
        Non-directional: same ionic attractions in all directions
        High melting and boiling points => solid at room temperature (because of strong electrostatic attractions)
        Fragile: disturbing the crystal can turn attractions into repulsions
        Can't conduce electricity
        Soluble in polar solvents
COVALENT BOND: bond in which atoms share electrons
    Usually between non-metals
    LEWIS MODEL: model that tries to explain chemical bonding by sharing valence electrons
        Simple and visual model, but limited
        Useful to study covalent bonds between main-group elements
        LEWIS DOT DIAGRAM: represents atoms with valence electrons as dots
    LONE ELECTRON PAIR: pair of valence electrons that are not shared (not part of any bond in the molecule)
    BOND ELECTRON PAIR: pair of valence electrons that are shared between two atoms
        Can be represented by a single line
    COVALENT MULTIPLE BOND: covalent bond where two atoms share more than one electron pair (vs. simple bond)
        Can be represented by multiple lines, one per electron pair
        Shorter bond distance than single bonds
    COORDINATE COVALENT BOND: covalent bond where all shared electrons are given by the same atom
    FORMAL CHARGE: for an atom in a covalent molecule, charge that the atom would have if the bond electrons are shared equally
        Formal charge = Valence electrons - (lone pairs + 1/2 * bonding electrons)
        The most likely Lewis structure is the one that has formal charge as closer to zero as possible
    RESONANCE STRUCTURES: set of Lewis diagrams that, together, describe a single real molecule
        None of the Lewis structures in the set exist in reality. The real molecule is something in-between them
        Used as a tool when Lewis structures fall short
        E.g. benzene described as two resonant structures with some double bonds in the ring, but all bonds have the same length
            Electrons that would create double bonds are shared between all the single bonds in the ring
    Not all molecules follow the octet rule, even within the main-group elements
        RADICAL: molecule/ion with an unpaired valence electron
            Total sum of valence electrons is odd => no way to satisfy octet rule
            Highly reactive
        Incomplete octets: one atom surrounded by less than 8 electrons, but the molecule is stable
        Expanded octets: one atom surrounded by more than 8 electrons, but the molecule is stable
            Requires 3d orbitals => the surrounded atom must be in period >= 3
    Bond polarity
        POLAR COVALENT BOND: bond where electron density is bigger around one atom than the others
        NON-POLAR COVALENT BOND: bond where electrons are shared almost equally by all the atoms
        DIPOLE MOMENT: describes the spatial distribution of charge of a system (its polarity)
            For two point charges, dipole moment = charge * distance (vector magnitude)
            A system with a non-zero dipole moment experiments a torque when subjected to an electric field
        A polar molecule has a dipole moment != 0
        Bond dipole moment: for a molecule with several bonds, the dipole moment of a single bond
            != 0 if the atoms have != electronegativity
        Total dipole moment = vector sum of all bond dipole moments
            A polyatomic molecule can be non-polar even with bond dipole moments != 0 (e.g. symmetry)
    VALENCE-SHELL ELECTRON-PAIR REPULSION MODEL (VSEPR): model that predicts molecular geometry from Lewis models
        Both bond and lone electron pairs contribute to geometry
        Electron pair geometry: the geometry of the pairs, considering lone pairs as if they were regular atoms
            Predicts molecular geometry
            2 pairs => linear
            3 pairs => trigonal planar
            4 pairs => tetrahedral (109.5º)
            5 pairs => trigonal bipyramidal
            6 pairs => octahedral
        Try to maximize the distance between electron pairs
        Multiple bonds are treated like single bonds
        If multiple central atoms are present, treat them independently
        When separating 3 regions of space: trigonal planar
        When separating 4 regions of space: tetrahedral (109.5º)
        The lone pairs cause more repulsion than the bond-forming pairs
            E.g. NH3 is not an exact tetrahedral
    VALENCE BOND THEORY: model that tries to explain chemical bonding by accommodating valence electrons in the atom's electronic orbitals
        Localized model: each valence electron is assigned to the orbitals of two particular atoms
        Valence electrons are shared because two orbitals overlap, in phase
        Explains more phenomena than Lewis' theory
        SIGMA BOND: covalent bond caused by two orbitals overlapping along the line joining the two nuclei
            The strongest type
            The most usual type for single bonds
        PI BOND: covalent bond caused by two orbitals overlapping outside the line joining the two nuclei
            Each orbital should have two lobes, and both lobes overlap laterally
            Weaker than sigma bonds
            In multiple bonds, only one can be sigma, the rest are pi
        Orbital hybridization: tries to explain molecular geometry with valence bond theory
            To form bonds, electrons become unpaired first
                E.g. C is [He] 2s² 2p² => becomes [He] 2s¹ 2p³ for bonding in CH4
            Symmetrical geometries form because some orbitals in the atom combine into a hybrid orbital when bonding
                E.g. CH4 is tetrahedral, so orbitals 2s¹ 2p³ hybridize into 4 hybrid sp³ orbitals
            Predict electron-pair geometry with VSEPR first, then deduce hybridization
                Linear =>               2 bonding orbitals => 2x sp    orbitals
                Trigonal planar =>      3 bonding orbitals => 3x sp²   orbitals
                Tetrahedral =>          4 bonding orbitals => 4x sp³   orbitals
                Trigonal bipyramidal => 5 bonding orbitals => 5x sp³d  orbitals
                Octahedral =>           6 bonding orbitals => 6x sp³d² orbitals

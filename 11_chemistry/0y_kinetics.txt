reaction rate: moles/s of reactants that become products
    V = -1/a * (d[A]/dt) where a is stechiometric coefficient of reactant A
rate law: rate of reaction = constant * (concentration of reactants)^n
    n: order of reaction
    Must be determined experimentally for each reaction
    Applies to the start of the reaction (?)
    each reactant has a reaction order, called partial reaction orders
    order of reaction must be an int, >= 0
    global reaction order = sum(partial reaction orders)
Integrated rate raw: change of concentration with time
Half-life time/half-reaction time (t_1/2): time that takes for a reaction to reduce the concentration to 1/2
    For a 1st order reaction, t_1/2 does not depend on [A]_0
    For a 2nd order reaction, it does
Collision theory: for a reaction to start you must have
    Molecules colliding
    The collision must be adequate
        They must have enough energy to start the reaction
        They should be properly oriented
    Rate proportional to the number of collisions by time
Activation energy: the minimal amount of energy by mole to initiate the reaction
    Even in an exothermic reaction, you need an activation energy, unrelated to ΔH
    This can be explained by considering that the products go into an intermediate state with higher energy than reactants or products
    If we reverse an exothermic reaction, we get an endothermic one. To activate it, we need the exothermic's Ea + ΔH
        So the endothermic one has always > Ea than its reverse exothermic

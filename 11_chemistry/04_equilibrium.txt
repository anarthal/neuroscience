Equilibrium is dynamic: even if macroscopically nothing changes, the reaction is proceeding in both directions
[X] denotes molar concentration
K_c: Equilibrium constant using concentrations
K_p: Equilibrium constant using partial pressures
Homogeneous Equilibrium: evth same phase
Subindex (aq): the reaction happens in solution
In gas phase, assuming ideal gases, K_p = K_c * (RT)^(Delta N)
In heterogeneous Equilibrium, solid and liquid species are not taken into account
    Their concentration is supposed to remain constant, given by their density
    Take only into account gases and species in solution
Equilibrium constants are adimensional even if you'd think otherwise
    Each concentration is supposed to be divided by a "reference concentration"
Reaction quotient Q: same definition as equilibrium constant, but usable outside equilibrium
    In equilibrium, Q = K
In Gibbs energy, use K_c for solution, K_p for gasses
Reaction direction
    K >> 1: shifted towards products
    K << 1: shifted towards reactants
Le Chatelier: if any variables involved in equilibrium (concentrations, temperature, pressure) changes, reaction shifts towards a new equilibrium
    "Tends to counteract the perturbation"
    Equilibrium constants don't change
    E.g. increasing reactant concentration also increases product concentration
    E.g. P increases (gas, constant T) => V decreases => [] increases for all species => effect depends on the stoichiometric coefficient
    But T changes K_c
        Endothermic: increasing T shifts towards products
        Exothermic: increasing T shifts towards reactants
    Catalyst makes equilibrium reached faster, but doesn't change K_c

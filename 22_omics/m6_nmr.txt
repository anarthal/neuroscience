NUCLEAR MAGNETIC RESONANCE (NMR): technique to analyze chemical compounds, based on the resonance frequencies of the atoms
Characteristics
    Can determine molecular structure (MS can't)
    Less sensitive than MS
    More reproducible than MS
    Non-destructive
    Less sample preparation. Can be used with intact tissue
Principle
    Atomic nuclei that have an odd number of protons and neutrons have spin and magnetic moment
        H1 and C13 have, C12 doesn't
    In the absence of a magnetic field, nuclei are randomly oriented
    If a constant magnetic field B0 is applied, nuclei align, with their magnetic moment direction precessing in parallel to B0
        Some in parallel (alpha state) => low energy state
        Some in antiparallel (beta state) => high energy state
        Transitioning between the low and high energy states requires energy
        LARMOR FREQUENCY: frequency of the precession movement. Proportional to B0
    If an orthogonal, magnetic field pulse is superimposed, nuclei are perturbed
        Nuclei absorb energy and flip to the high-energy state
        When they return to equilibrium, they emit the energy back as electromagnetic radiation at their Larmor frequency
        FREE INDUCTION DECAY (FID): signal emitted by the perturbed nuclei when returning to equilibrium, captured by NMR equipment
Output: spectrum with frequency in X axis, intensity in Y axis
    CHEMICAL SHIFT (delta): measurement of frequency relative to a reference molecule (ppm)
        delta = (freq_compound - freq_reference) / freq_instrument
        Because frequencies depend on B0 => instrument-dependent
        Depends on the electronic environment of each nucleus (proton for H1)
        TMS used as a standard
        CHEMICALLY EQUIVALENT NUCLEI: nuclei experimenting the same chemical shift
        Electrons surrounding a nucleus create a magnetic field and shield the nucleus => having electrons around reduces chemical shift
        Electronegative nuclei draw electrons from nearby nuclei => de-shield => bigger chemical shift
        Hydrogen bonds => de-shield (dynamic)
    INTENSITY INTEGRATION: area under the curve for each peak
        Depends on the number of nuclei with that particular electronic environment
    SPIN-SPIN COUPLING (J): split of peaks corresponding to protons due to other neighboring protons
        Num_peaks = n + 1, with n = number of neighbor protons
        Singlet: n = 0, no splitting
        Doublet: n = 1, split into 2
        Triplet: n = 2, split into 3
        Quartet: n = 3, split into 4
        No splitting if the neighboring group is chemically equivalent
2D NUCLEAR MAGNETIC RESONANCE (2D-NMR): NMR experiment repeated under two difference excitation conditions => outputs 2 frequency axes
C13 NMR yields a smaller signal
ISOTOPIC LABELLING: label a molecule by replacing some of its atoms by detectable isotopes (e.g. C13)

EPIGENOMICS: genome-wide study of epigenetic markers
    Answers 'how is DNA being used?'
    Cell-type specific, dynamic, with environmental influence
Markers
    DNA methylation in promoters: associated with repression
        Happens in the C at CpG sites
        The one with most long-term effects, but can still change through lifetime
        Acts as a signal to recruit proteins that stick histone markers and close DNA. Does not block DNA directly
        Methylation in the exon does NOT cause silencing
    Histone modifications
        Shorter-term effects than DNA methylation
        Acts as a signal to recruit proteins that open/close DNA. Does not block DNA directly
        Learning changes histone marks
        H3K4me3: active promoter
        H3K27ac: active enhancer
        H3K9ac: open chromatin
        H3K27me3: repression
        H3K9me3: constitutive heterochromatin
    Chromatin accessibility: physical outcome of epigenetics
        Closed chromatin: gene is silent
        Permissive chromatin: intermediate state between open and closed
        Open chromatin: gene is transcribed
Study methods
    BISULFITE SEQUENCING: technique to study DNA methylation across the genome
        DNA is extracted and fragmented into a library
        Sodium bisulfite converts unmethylated C into U. Methylated C are left as C
        PCR amplification and sequencing => U amplified as T
        After alignment, compare original sequences with sequences amplified after bisulfite conversion
            Original C, converted C => C was methylated
            Original C, converted T => C was unmethylated
            Original T, converted T => this has always been a T, unrelated to methylation
    CHROMATIN IMMUNOPRECIPITATION SEQUENCING (ChIP-seq): technique to study histone marks across the genome
        Select an antibody targeting a specific histone mark
        DNA is extracted, cross-linked (prevent nucleosomes from disassembling) and fragmented
        Immunoprecipitation: with antibodies, purify the histones containing the selected mark and its associated DNA
        Clean up DNA and sequence => the DNA that appears in sequencing is the one containing the mark
    ASSAY FOR TRANSPOSASE-ACCESSIBLE CHROMATIN (ATAC-seq): technique to study which parts of the genome are open chromatin
        Cells are lysed gently (intact nucleus)
        TAGMENTATION: process where a transposase enzyme cuts DNA and inserts sequencing adapters, in a single step
        A transposase enzyme enters the nucleus and performs tagmentation only where chromatin is open
        Select DNA fragments containing the adapters only
        Sequence them => the DNA that appears in sequencing is the one where chromatin is open
    Unbiased: none of these methods require targeting a particular sequence
    Combine these methods with GWAS
        GWAS: set of SNPs that might be important. Look up epigenetic profiles for these loci
        ATAC-seq: is the chromatin open at this locus? In which cell types?
        ChIP-seq: what kind of element is the locus? Enhancer, promoter?
        Bisulfite sequencing: does the locus show long-term repression
POISED GENE: gene that is not active, but is in a ready state so it can be activated quickly
SUPER-ENHANCER: cluster of enhancers close in the genome

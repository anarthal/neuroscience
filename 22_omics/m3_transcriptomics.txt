TRANSCRIPTOMICS: large scale analysis of the transcriptome (gene expression)
VOLCANO PLOT: plots statistical significance vs. magnitude of change (fold change) between two conditions
GENE ONTOLOGY: DB with a classification of genes based on functionality
mRNA MICRO-ARRAY: measures gene expression for a big number of genes
    Yes/no check for a number of genes => needs knowledge about the sequence
    Cheap, high throughput
BULK TRANSCRIPTOMICS: measures gene expression averaged across all cells in the sample
    RNA-seq: retro-transcribe, PCR, sequence as DNA
    Can measure gene expression differences between != conditions (e.g. healthy vs. disease)
    Blender effect: looses cell-specifics and effects happening in a small subset of the cells
    Can be used to discover new transcripts
SINGLE-CELL RNA SEQUENCING (scRNA-seq): measures gene expression of individual cells
    Gel Beads-in-emulsions (GEMs): technology to achieve single-cell resolution by isolating each cell in a bead
        Each bead has a != barcode: sequence that uniquely identifies each cell. Attaches to the poly-A mRNA tail
    UNIQUE MOLECULE IDENTIFIER (UMI): sequence that uniquely identifies each mRNA molecule in scRNA-seq
        PCR may amplify each molecule differently => deduplicate => truly know how much RNA there was
    Expensive
    High dimensionality => more difficult to analyze
    RNA PSEUDOTIME: family of algorithms that try to infer cell development across time
        Based on a snapshot acquired at a given time => does not represent real time
        Good for tissues in homeostasis (e.g. skin)
        Requires specifying the initial and final states => may introduce bias
    RNA VELOCITY: family of algorithms that infer how cells will evolve along pseudotime trajectories
        Based on the ratio of spliced vs. unspliced RNA for each gene
        States with more spliced RNA come after the ones with more unspliced RNA
    Lineage tracing: find which cells arise from a given ancestor by mitosis
        Tags each cell with a unique barcode, allows for development, collects the sample
    Multiome: perform RNA-seq and epigenomic analysis in single cells
        Answers 'is this gene expressed because of this other epigenetic marker?'
Spatial RNA-seq: measure gene expression keeping spatial information
    Collect a number of small samples from a tissue slice and record spatial information. Perform RNA-seq to all of them
    Looses resolution: every point contains more than one cell
Dimensionality reduction
    PCA: for low-dimensionality (~500)
        Separate by principal components
        Each PC may be assigned a 'label' (e.g. what is the biological function?)
        Distance between points is relevant
        Explained variance tells about how good the PC is
    UNIFIED MANIFOLD APPROXIMATION AND PROJECTION (UMAP): non-linear dimensionality reduction algorithm for high-dimensionality data
        Distances are not relevant, axis are meaningless
        Just for visualization, "close" points are similar, nothing more
Clustering is performed independently of dimensionality reduction
    When the clustering algorithm yields results consistent with UMAP, that's a good sign
    "Separation" depends on context: if we pick neurons vs. astrocytes, we can't see differences between astrocytes. But we can see them if we pick astrocytes alone
EXPRESSION QUANTITATIVE TRAIT LOCUS (eQTL): genetic variant that influences how much a certain gene is expressed
    Usually located within promoters/enhancers
    cis-eQTL: if the regulatory effect happens in cis (vs. trans-eQTL)
    Discovered by acquiring genotype and gene expression levels for many individuals => statistical analysis ('does this variant predict expression levels?')
